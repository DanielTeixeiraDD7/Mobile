Cron expression helper

Check what a cron schedule really means before you deploy it, and when it will run next.

Runs locally: your input never leaves this page.

~/bitbench/cron-expression local

minute (0–59) · hour (0–23) · day of month (1–31) · month (1–12 or JAN–DEC) · day of week (0–7 or SUN–SAT, 0 and 7 = Sunday)

Supported syntax

The classic 5-field format of crontab (Vixie cron, cronie), also used by many CI and cloud schedulers:

┌ minute (0–59)
│ ┌ hour (0–23)
│ │ ┌ day of month (1–31)
│ │ │ ┌ month (1–12 or JAN–DEC)
│ │ │ │ ┌ day of week (0–7 or SUN–SAT; 0 and 7 are Sunday)
* * * * *
  • * any value, 1-5 range, 1,15 list, */10 step, 8-18/2 stepped range.
  • Macros: @hourly, @daily (@midnight), @weekly, @monthly, @yearly (@annually).

Not every scheduler uses the same syntax

  • Quartz and Spring use 6 or 7 fields (with seconds and a year) and special characters such as ? L W #: not supported here.
  • Some schedulers number days of the week from 1, or run in UTC regardless of your time zone: check your platform’s documentation.
  • When both day of month and day of week are restricted, classic cron runs on days matching either field: 0 0 1 * 1 runs on the 1st of the month and on every Monday. This tool follows that rule.

Next runs

The next five times are computed in your browser’s time zone or in UTC. Around daylight saving changes, real cron daemons may skip or repeat runs differently.
